Who ChatGPT recommends for outdoor and travel gear

We asked ChatGPT 20 questions the way a US shopper would, from ultralight tents to carry-on backpacks, and logged two things: which products it named, and which store it sent the buyer to.

What we found

1. Specialist questions belong to independent brands

When the question comes from an enthusiast (an ultralight tent under 2 lb, a backpacking quilt, a full hammock setup), ChatGPT names small specialist brands almost exclusively: Durston, Zpacks, Six Moon Designs, Neve Gear, Featherstone, Warbonnet, Dutchware. It also links to those brands' own sites. These brands are reviewed and discussed in depth by the communities that use them, which likely gives the assistant plenty to draw on.

2. Everyday and budget questions go to the big names

For everyday products with a budget in the question (a carry-on backpack under $300, a work backpack under $200, a suitcase under $300), the list fills with large brands such as Osprey, Patagonia, Thule, Samsonite and The North Face, and the links go to REI and Amazon. These are the questions most shoppers actually ask.

The budget alone can change who gets named. One independent travel brand was ChatGPT's first pick for "best travel backpack if money is no object", but it was missing from both "under $300" versions of the question, even though it sells a 40L pack for $250.

3. Being recommended is not the same as getting the sale

In two answers, ChatGPT recommended a product from a direct-to-consumer brand but linked the buyer to a retailer to buy it: a camera backpack linked to two camera stores, and an insulated sleeping pad linked to REI. The brand earned the recommendation; the retailer got the click. When we checked, neither brand's own product page showed customer reviews.

4. At the cheapest end, Amazon-only brands fill the gap

For "beginner camping hammock with bug net under $100", four of the six picks were brands sold mainly on Amazon. Well-known hammock brands were not named at all. When no established brand clearly owns a price point, the gap gets filled by whoever sells most visibly at that price.

What this means for independent brands: state the price band, the size and the use case in plain words on your product pages, show your reviews in your product data, and answer the comparison questions shoppers ask. An assistant can only match your product to a question if your page says what the question says.

How we tested

We asked each question in ChatGPT with web results turned on, phrased as a US shopper would ask it, in November 2026. We recorded every product named, its position and the stores linked. Each question was asked once for this study.

AI answers change between runs and over time, and ChatGPT does not publish how it picks products or stores, so read this as a snapshot of patterns, not a fixed ranking. In client work we repeat each question several times and report how often a brand appears, not a single answer.

No brand paid to be included, and we are not affiliated with any brand or store named here.
